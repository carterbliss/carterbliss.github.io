My long-term aim is to specialize in hardware and chip design — understanding how transistors, analog circuits, and digital logic are physically realized on silicon. That's where I want to end up. But getting there means building the broadest possible foundation first.

At Georgia Tech, I'm actively seeking experience across the full hardware stack: embedded system design, PCB design, robotics and firmware, and designing processors. The wider my exposure now, the stronger I'll be when I do narrow in. I'm concentrating in Electronic Devices and Circuit Technology, with a planned minor in Computer Science — the software and architectural layer that silicon serves is part of the picture too.

Outside the classroom, I'm gaining that hands-on experience through the organizations I'm part of. On HyTech Racing, I contribute to Georgia Tech's electric racecar team. As an Advanced Prototyping Technical Operator at the Hive Makerspace, I train staffers on fabrication tools. Through my VIP team, I'm designing an autonomous testing system for supercapacitors on a NASA balloon mission — my most direct IC design exposure so far. I'm also a member of Silicon Jackets, GT's chip design club.

I'm currently seeking a summer 2027 internship in hardware, embedded systems, or software engineering. Longer term, I want to be someone hardware and systems teams can rely on, and I'm interested in eventually pursuing a master's in Electrical Engineering.
