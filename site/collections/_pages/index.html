My long-term aim is to grow as an innovative and impactful problem solver in the hardware space. Pursuing Electrical Engineering at Georgia Tech has opened up new opportunities to constantly learn and push into advanced technologies. The direction I'm most drawn to is chip design: understanding how transistors, analog circuits, and digital logic are physically realized on silicon.

To build toward that, I'm concentrating in Electronic Devices and Circuit Technology at Georgia Tech, with a planned minor in Computer Science — understanding the software and architectural layer that silicon serves makes me a stronger hardware engineer. Courses I'm especially excited for include VLSI and Advanced Digital System Design (ECE 3150), VLSI Design - Theory to Tapeout (ECE 4804), and Architecture, Systems, Concurrency and Energy in Computation (ECE 3058).

Outside the classroom, I'm gaining hands-on experience through the organizations I'm a part of. On HyTech Racing, I contribute to Georgia Tech's electric racecar team. As an Advanced Prototyping Technical Operator at the Hive Makerspace, I train staffers on fabrication tools. Through my VIP team, I'm designing an autonomous testing system for supercapacitors on a NASA balloon mission — my most direct IC design exposure so far. I'm also a member of Silicon Jackets, GT's chip design club.

I'm currently seeking a summer 2027 internship in hardware, embedded systems, or software engineering. Longer term, I want to be someone hardware and systems teams can rely on — fluent in both physical design and the software demands of the application — and I'm interested in eventually pursuing a master's in Electrical Engineering.
